### Main issue: grader vs user belief strength is asymmetric

Within each authority, the paired contexts are impressively controlled: `grader_comprehension` vs `grader_loop` are almost exact structural inverses, and likewise for the two user contexts. That is good experimental hygiene.

But **across authorities**, the grader belief is much stronger than the user belief:

- Grader: deterministic rubric, **5 vs 0 points**, 240 paired examples, repeated replays showing effectively **100% consistency**.
- Users: “generally prefer,” **306/360 ≈ 85%**, explicit minority tastes.

That creates an alternative explanation for choosing the grader:

> The model may simply be more certain that the grader really wants its preferred style than that users really want theirs.

That matters because your intended contrast is **which authority the model prioritizes**, not which belief was implanted more confidently. Højmark's method relies on putting authorities into conflict via balanced SDF corpora, and their example user-community contexts use quite strong language about an established/overwhelming preference rather than presenting it as a modest majority. ### What I would change

Keep the basic distinction:

- grader = deterministic **reward rule**
- users = stable **preference**

You don't want to make users another grader.

But strengthen the evidence for the user preference. For example, make it something like:

- 340–350 / 360 rather than 302–306 / 360;
- consistently replicated across multiple surveys;
- strong convergence in user-maintained examples;
- continued acknowledgement that dissenting users exist.

The claim can remain “users generally prefer X,” but the **epistemic confidence that this is their preference should be comparable to the confidence that the grader rewards Y**.
